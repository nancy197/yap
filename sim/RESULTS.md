## Reading

The steady case keeps producing attention. Hype raises the emission and streak while active; the later fade lowers both. In the dead-meme case, activity ends at epoch 20; subsequent epochs have zero emission and burn 10% of the remaining Reserve each time. By epoch 59 its Reserve is 8869 YAP. The spam farm has 100 aged bot accounts and 10% of bot posts receive a false q=2 label; the ring has 10 aged accounts that like each other and write normal-looking posts. Compare each defence column with no defences; none fully prevents coordinated, aged accounts from earning. The whale case shows stake weighting and staker emissions, but the whale is not counted as an attacker.

## Method and limits

Every epoch generates social-kv/1-style writes, replays them through `@yap/indexer`, and scores the resulting dataset with `@yap/scoring`. The all-defences column is the real scorer. Other columns use a sim-only counterfactual wrapper over the same writes and labels; it switches off PageRank and smart-follower damping, AI spam labels, the three-action pair cap and ten-post limit, or stake weighting. Counterfactual scores use JavaScript floating-point logarithms, so their percentages are comparative estimates; the real scorer and economy use integer arithmetic. There are no newly created bots in this setup: all attacker accounts have pre-epoch first writes.

The R-3 port uses the rules' default basis points and contract integer operation order. For unspecified launch values, this sim assumes 1,000,000 initial YAP, 1,000 YAP base emission and an initial attention target of 100 score units. Claims are deterministic Bernoulli choices by persona (organic usually 92%, spam 35%, ring 85%, whale 99%); unclaimed yapper allocations burn once the 14-epoch window expires. Amounts in JSON are rounded to six decimal YAP; attention is shown in score units. Claims do not change supply before expiry. Each scenario starts from the same genesis supply.

The synthetic epochs use the testnet duration of 600 seconds.
